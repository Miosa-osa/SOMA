# SOMA community code of conduct

## Our commitment

SOMA by MIOSA is committed to an open, respectful, technically rigorous community where people can participate without harassment or intimidation.
We value clear evidence, patient explanation, honest disagreement, and criticism directed at ideas and implementations rather than people.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful and specific in reviews, issues, discussions, and public project spaces.
- Assume good intent while still checking security, correctness, performance, and provenance claims carefully.
- Give credit for research, code, reports, and ideas.
- Welcome contributors with different backgrounds and levels of virtualization experience.
- Disclose conflicts of interest that could affect a technical or governance decision.
- Respect coordinated vulnerability disclosure and private incident handling.
- Stop behavior that a maintainer identifies as disruptive or unsafe.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, discrimination, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal attacks, insults, doxxing, or publication of private information without permission.
- Deliberate intimidation, sustained disruption, or bad-faith manipulation of project processes.
- Knowingly false attribution, fabricated measurements, plagiarism, or concealment of provenance.
- Publishing a vulnerability, exploit, secret, or private report in violation of a coordinated disclosure request.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a conduct or security concern in good faith.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, release channels, project events, and private project communication.
It also applies when someone officially represents SOMA or the MIOSA organization.

## Reporting

Report a conduct concern privately to the repository maintainers through a private contact method published by the `Miosa-osa` GitHub organization.
Do not open a public issue when doing so would expose a target, private information, or a security report.
Include the relevant links, dates, participants, and context that maintainers need to evaluate the report.

If the report concerns one maintainer, another maintainer who is not involved must handle it.
Maintainers will share the report only with people required to investigate and respond.
No fixed response-time guarantee is offered during pre-alpha, but good-faith reports will be acknowledged when maintainers are available.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, issue a private warning, restrict participation, temporarily suspend access, or permanently ban participation.
The response should be proportionate to severity, impact, repetition, intent, and the safety of affected people.
Maintainers should document material enforcement decisions privately and avoid unnecessary disclosure.

Good-faith disagreement with a technical decision is not a conduct violation.
Harassment or retaliation disguised as technical criticism is a conduct violation.
